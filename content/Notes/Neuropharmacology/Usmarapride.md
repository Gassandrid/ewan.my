CNS-selective [[5-HT4 receptor|5-HT4]] partial agonist, designed as a brain-penetrant successor to Prucalopride. Prucalopride was validated in healthy people across three independent tasks: RAVLT (verbal recall), PILT (probabilistic learning), and emotional memory recognition, all showing significant improvement [@murphyRole5HT4Receptors2020]. The problem was diarrhea from peripheral cAMP activity. Usmarapride fixes this with selective CNS penetration.

Mechanism: 5-HT4 agonism drives [[hippocampus|hippocampal]] [[cAMP]], which triggers [[BDNF]] release, dendritic growth, and [[neurogenesis]]. Also reduces hippocampal excitability and blocks depotentiation, raising the [[Long Term Potentiation]] encoding threshold while protecting established traces. The pro-cognitive effect is hippocampus-specific: Prucalopride showed no effect on working memory or implicit contextual learning, which are PFC-dependent tasks [@murphyRole5HT4Receptors2020]. For full cognitive coverage pair with [[Tropisetron]], [[Neboglamine]], an M1 ligand, or [[TAK-653]].

new context sharpened this: 5-HT4 agonists increase cortical [[Acetylcholine]] and histamine, potentiate donepezil-induced ACh release, and increase hippocampal theta power. The dentate-gyrus finding is also very on-theme: 5-HT4 partial agonism prevents depotentiation, so the effect is less “more memory now” and more “freshly encoded traces are harder to dissolve.”

The hippocampal excitability reduction is the anxiolytic mechanism. Hippocampal neurogenesis accounts for the antidepressant effects. synergy; Usmarapride drives hippocampal BDNF release, [[ACD-856]] sensitizes TrkB for it. Mechanistically tight and subjectively strong. Phase 1 class-level data from SUVN-D4010 (same 5-HT4 agonist class): generally safe, with relatively high headache occurrence above 15mg [@nirogiFirstinHumanStudiesEvaluate2021]. Hard ceiling there.

stress-resilience angle: prucalopride / PF-04995274 / RS67333 data suggests 5-HT4 partial agonism can act prophylactically against learned fear and stress-induced depressive behavior, with p11 as a relevant 5-HT4 trafficking/signaling cofactor ([stress prophylaxis review](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6969048/)).

#### 1. [Déjà-vu? Neural and behavioural effects of the 5-HT4 receptor agonist, prucalopride, in a hippocampal-dependent memory task](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8488034/)

1.1 In an fMRI study of 44 humans, 6d of treatment with Prucalopride significantly improved recall on previously seen neutral images and distinguishing them from new images. Prucalopride bilaterally increased activity in the hippocampal and right angular gyrus. Prucalopride did not change state anxiety or affect, and did not differ from placebo for side effects. Prucalopride improved memory performance in the post-scan recall task: participants in the Prucalopride group were more accurate in selecting items seen before vs distractors, and also distinguishing each category of image. Prucalopride increased BOLD fMRI signal intensity in the right and left hippocampi in response to both novel and familiar images. Prucalopride increased activity in the right angular gyrus.

1.2 5-HT4 is an excitatory postsynaptic G protein-coupled receptor (GPCR) expressed in brain areas involved in emotion and cognition, such as the basal ganglia, hippocampus, amygdala, and prefrontal cortex (PFC). 5-HT4 agonists improve performance during learning and memory tasks, with obvious effects after only a few doses. This is particularly apparent in tasks tapping hippocampal-dependent learning and memory. This likely relates to various downstream effects of 5-HT4 agonism including increased hippocampal cell proliferation and promotion of learning-induced spine growth, induction of LTP in the hippocampus, increased release of neurotransmitters such as acetylcholine (ACh) in the hippocampus and frontal cortex, and increased expression of BDNF. Cortical release of ACh appears to increase the power of hippocampal Theta oscillations following 5-HT4 receptor agonism.

1.3 A single 1mg dose of Prucalopride has pro-cognitive effects across 3 different learning and memory tasks, namely, the Rey Auditory Verbal Learning Task (RAVLT), the Probabilistic Instrumental Learning Task (PILT), and emotional memory as part of the Emotional Test Battery (ETB).

1.4 Pretreatment with 5-HT4 agonists before memory encoding improves learning and memory in animals, with evidence of the effect enduring with repeated, 14d administration.

1.5 Prucalopride appears to indirectly reduce bursts of AMPAR-mediated currents in the hippocampus, altering glutamatergic transmission.

1.6 GABAergic and glutamatergic neurons are thought to mediate the increased release of ACh that occurs with 5-HT4 receptor agonism.

1.7 Human PET studies have found an inverse correlation between verbal memory scores and 5-HT4 receptor binding in the hippocampus and more globally. This is because the binding competes with endogenous serotonin (5-HT) for binding at 5-HT4.

1.8 In first-episode psychosis, the volumetric reduction in hippocampal subfields appears to correlate significantly with 5-HT4 receptor density.

#### 2. [Modulation of memory processes and cellular excitability in the dentate gyrus of freely moving rats by a 5-HT4 receptors partial agonist, and an antagonist](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0028390804002266?via%3Dihub)

2.1 Systemic injection of a 5-HT4 partial agonist prior to the induction of LTP in the dentate gyrus (DG) by high-frequency stimulation was followed by a population spike increase.

2.2 5-HT4 plays a major role in learning and memory. 5-HT4 is positively coupled to adenylate cyclase (AC). Activation of 5-HT4 leads to an increase in cAMP, which then activates PKA. The activation of PKA induces long-lasting (2h) inhibition of outward, delayed rectifying K+ currents due to a long-lasting blockade of phosphatases. 5-HT4 receptors are heterogeneously located in the CNS, with high concentrations in numerous structures involved in learning and memory such as the hippocampus and regions of the cortex. In the hippocampus, they are localized in pyramidal neurons. There is evidence of a loss of 5-HT4 receptor numbers in Alzheimer’s Disease (AD), both in the cortex (–23%), and more prominently in the hippocampus (–66%).

2.3 Selective 5-HT4 agonists have been shown to enhance cognition in the delayed-match-to-sample (DMTS) task in primates, and reverse Atropine-induced deficit in the Morris water maze (MWM) in rats. 5-HT4 agonists improve long-term memory in normal adult rats. A potent, selective 5-HT4 agonist showed a promnestic effect on several learning and memory tests in adult, aged, and/or Scopolamine-treated mice.

2.4 LTP in the hippocampus and DG of freely moving rats can be modulated by 5-HT4. 

2.5 In an electrophysiological study, a 5-HT4 agonist enhanced population spike amplitude in the hippocampal CA1 field potential evoked by Schaffer-collateral stimulation and also augmented tetanus-induced LTP.

2.6 A 5-HT4 partial agonist showed powerful effects on DG synaptic plasticity by prevention of depotentiation, which led to reinforcement of LTP.

2.7 In an olfactory associative task, a 5-HT4 partial agonist injected 30min before a 3rd training session was followed by improvement in learning and memory of the odor-reward associations (declarative memory) that were not due to modulation of motor activity.

#### 4. [Prophylactic efficacy of 5-HT4R agonists against stress](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6969048/)

4.1 In 129S6/SvEv mice, a 5-HT4 partial agonist attenuated learned fear in male, but not female mice. The same compound prevented anxiety-like behavior in both sexes.

4.2 Prucalopride and another 5-HT4 partial agonist attenuated learned fear and decreased stress-induced depressive-like behavior. Electrophysiological studies showed that both Prucalopride and Ketamine attenuate bursts of large-amplitude AMPAR-mediated synaptic transmission in CA3.

4.3 5-HT4 partial agonists are effective prophylactics against stress.

4.4 5-HT4 receptors are highly expressed in the periphery, including the heart and adrenal glands. It is also expressed in the CNS, in areas such as the amygdala, medial PFC (mPFC), nucleus accumbens (NAcc), and hippocampus.

4.5 5-HT4 receptor knockout (KO) mice display increased anxiety-like and depressive-like behavior.

4.6 5-HT4 agonists stimulate neurogenesis in the hippocampus and induce rapid-acting antidepressant effects.

4.7 A 5-HT4 partial agonist, RS67333, is effective in improving behavioral deficits, decreasing the number of amyloid plaques as well as amyloid beta (Abeta) species, and decreasing hippocampal astrogliosis and microgliosis in the 5xFAD mouse model of AD.

4.8 Prucalopride is a 5-HT4 agonist. In 2018, it was approved by the FDA for chronic constipation, and is currently being tested for chronic intestinal pseudo-obstruction.

4.9 PF-04995274, a 5-HT4 partial agonist, was evaluated alone or in combination with Donepezil, on Scopolamine-induced deficits in psychomotor and cognitive function in healthy adults.

4.10 RS67333, Prucalopride, and PF-04995274 attenuate learned fear. RS67333 prevents depressive-like behavior when administered chronically, and stress-induced anxiety-like behavior in both sexes when administered acutely. Prucalopride and PF-04995274 decrease stress-induced depressive-like behavior in the forced swim test (FST).

4.11 RS67333 1.5mg/kg/d beginning 3w prior to 6w of Corticosterone treatment decreased the Corticosterone-induced gain in body weight. It was anxiolytic in the elevated plus maze (EPM).

4.12 A single, prophylactic injection of RS67333 attenuates learned fear and protects against stress-induced hypophagia in male and female mice.

4.13 A single, prophylactic injection of Prucalopride or PF-04995274 is protective against stress in male mice.

4.14 Ketamine and Prucalopride exhibit a common mechanism by reducing bursts of large AMPAR-driven synaptic currents in CA3.

4.15 5-HT4 receptors interact with the Ca2+ effector protein p11. 5-HT4 is highly co-expressed with p11. p11 increases surface expression of 5-HT4 in the hippocampus and amygdala, facilitates its downstream signaling pathways, and is necessary for the antidepressant effects of 5-HT4 receptor agonists. Levels of p11 are correlated with measures of suicidality and PTSD, indicating its potential as a biomarker for suicidal ideation and PTSD.

4.16 5-HT4 expression and activity in the PFC are regulated by casein kinase 2 (CK2), which may be an important modulator of depressive- and anxiety-like behaviors.

4.17 Microbiota in the enteric nervous system (ENS) communicate with the CNS by stimulating 5-HT4 receptors present throughout the gut to stimulate 5-HT release in the brain.

#### 7. [The 5-Hydroxytryptamine4 Receptor Agonists Prucalopride and PRX-03140 Increase Acetylcholine and Histamine Levels in the Rat Prefrontal Cortex and the Power of Stimulated Hippocampal θ Oscillations](https://jpet.aspetjournals.org/article/S0022-3565\(24\)18230-4/fulltext)

7.1 5-HT4 agonists stimulate brain ACh release.

7.2 In vitro, Prucalopride bound to native rat brain 5-HT4 receptors with a Ki of 30nM, and increased cAMP production in HEK-293 cells expressing recombinant rat 5-HT4 receptors. In vivo receptor occupancy studies established that Prucalopride was able to cross the blood-brain barrier (BBB) and bound to 5-HT4 receptors in the rat brain, with an EC50 of 330nM. #### 8. [The serotonergic 5-HT4 receptor: A unique modulator of hippocampal synaptic information processing and cognition](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1074742716300892)

8.1 The 5-HT4 receptor may contribute significantly to cognitive function and regulates less ostensible aspects of brain function: it engages metaplastic regulation of synaptic responsiveness in key brain structures such as the hippocampus, specifically promoting persistent forms of synaptic plasticity, and influences the direction of change in synaptic strength in selected hippocampal subfields. 

8.2 5-HT4 receptors inhibit biochemical cascades that lead to AD. 5-HT4 expression is reduced in AD patients.

8.3 5-HT4 receptors are localized postsynaptically in the hippocampus. Activation results in increased neuronal excitability via activation of AC. This leads to an increase in PKA activity and cAMP levels, leading to the inhibition of K+ channels. These actions are mediated by G-protein signaling. Activation of 5-HT4 reduces afterhyperpolarization currents in CA1 pyramidal cells that contributes to increased excitability. 5-HT4 additionally signals independently of G-protein signaling, such as phosphorylation of the 5-HT4-associated non-receptor tyrosine kinase Src, that leads to activation of ERK. ERK in turn is critically involved in hippocampal synaptic plasticity, most specifically LTD of synaptic efficacy. 5-HT4 receptor agonism also stimulates hippocampal expression of plasticity/learning-related proteins such as BDNF, Akt, CREB expression, as well as neurogenesis in the DG.

8.4 In addition to direct effects on neurons, 5-HT4 indirectly influences neuronal activity via modulation of the release of other neurotransmitters, such as GABA, ACh, or dopamine (DA).

8.5 Abeta peptides accumulate in the AD brain and are believed to play a major role in AD neurodegeneration. Abeta is generated by cleavage of Amyloid precursor protein (APP) by beta- and gamma-Secretases. Another APP processing pathway, the non-amyloidogenic pathway, leads to the creation of the extracellular soluble fragment of APP, sAPPalpha, which impedes the generation of Abeta via activation of alpha- and gamma-Secretases. Additionally, sAPPalpha is neurotrophic and neuroprotective, and may have cognitive enhancing effects. Activation of 5-HT4 is important in promoting the necessary steps resulting in the cleavage of APP and the subsequent release of sAPPalpha. 

8.6 At the level of cognition, multiple studies on 5-HT4 receptors have revealed an intrinsic role for this receptor in a large variety of behavioral learning tasks. 5-HT4 receptors play an important role in hippocampus-dependent learning and memory. Agonism at 5-HT4 supports cognitive enhancement.

8.7 5-HT4 receptors have been proposed as neuronal markers of learning and memory: active cognitive processing results in increased expression of 5-HT4 in the corresponding brain regions. Human hippocampal 5-HT4 expression levels correlate with memory performance.

8.8 Animals with their learning impaired by a mAChR antagonist were tested in a linear maze task, or in a spatial water maze. They showed impaired performance that was eliminated by a 5-HT4 agonist.

8.9 5-HT4 receptor agonists enhance place and object recognition in young animals, when a 5-HT4 agonist was administered IP prior to the acquisition phase. Dose sensitivity changed with age, with older animals requiring higher doses in order to improve place and object recognition. Object recognition was improved when the 5-HT4 agonist was applied in the consolidation phase, suggesting that 5-HT4 agonism impacts information processing differently, relative to the stages of rodent maturation and development, and indicating that 5-HT4 may modulate both acquisition and consolidation components of object-place memory.

8.10 5-HT4 receptor agonists prevent the age-induced loss of reference memory.

8.11 In naïve animals who have not undergone a learning experience, 5-HT4 agonism results in dendritic spine growth, which is positively related to short- and long-term synaptic plasticity. These effects are supported by 5-HT4-mediated hippocampal neurogenesis and stimulation of BDNF expression.

8.12 The positive effects of 5-HT4 on memory are not restricted to hippocampus-dependent tasks: in a social olfactory memory test, injection of a 5-HT4 agonist immediately after the first presentation of an unfamiliar juvenile rat to an adult rat, improved short-term memory such that the adult rat recognized the juvenile even after a 2h delay. This memory-enhancing effect was counteracted by an IP injection of a 5-HT4 receptor antagonist.

8.13 RS67333, given under exactly the same conditions to rats, has no impact on LTP (>24h), suppresses depotentiation, and prevents LTD (>24h) in the CA1 region. This provoked the interesting possibility that activation of 5-HT4 skews information encoding toward processes that require and recruit LTP. Strikingly, although 10Hz afferent stimulation resulted in no significant change in synaptic strength, the same stimulation given in the presence of a 5-HT4 antagonist resulted in LTD, while a 5-HT4 agonist led to synaptic potentiation.

8.14 In the DG, 5-HT4 antagonism prolongs LTD and has no effect on LTP, whereas 5-HT4 agonism prevents LTD and curtails LTP but also prevents depotentiation in behaving rats. This suggests that, in the DG, LTP is promoted at the expense of LTD, LTP is also constrained within a physiological range via 5-HT4 agonism.  
8.15 At mossy fiber synapses of the CA3 region, 5-HT4 agonism prevents both LTP and LTD in behaving rats, indicating that under 5-HT4 agonistic conditions, long-term information storage (LTP/LTD) at CA3 synapses is prevented, whereas information storage by means of LTP at both CA1 and DG synapses is promoted.

8.16 In the CA1 of behaving rats, agonist activation of 5-HT4 prevents learning-facilitated LTD along with the spatial learning that was associated with the robust prolongation of LTD. Activation of 5-HT4 also prevents learning-induced depotentiation of LTP in the CA1 region. This suggests that 5-HT4 receptors are involved in prioritizing the forms of memory that depend on LTP (e.g. contextual or associative fear experiences, novel exposure to new space and/or global changes in space) over forms of memory that depend on LTD (e.g. encoding and updating novel spatial content, even in an environment that previously facilitated the expression of robust LTP).

8.17 Multiple isoforms of the 5-HT4 receptor have been identified in both rat and human brains. In the hippocampus, the relative distribution of mRNA for the 5-HT4a and 5-HT4e splice variants was reported as being similar in the DG and CA regions, but 5-HT4b mRNA expression was stronger in the DG than CA regions. There is a high density of 5-HT4 in the stratum lucidum of the CA3 region, which could explain why receptor agonism so potently affected both mossy fiber LTP and mossy fiber LTD in this structure.

8.18 5-HT4 receptors may support optimal and appropriate synaptic information storage of the most salient aspects of spatial and associative experiences. Maintaining synaptic plasticity within a functional range may be one of the functions that 5-HT4 supports.

8.19 In general, pre-training administration of a 5-HT4 agonist results in an enhanced ability to learn, whereas post-training injection of a 5-HT4 agonist impairs memory consolidation. Although 5-HT4 agonism 2h after induction of LTP or LTD has no effect on established plasticity in mossy fiber synapses, and does not affect LTD in the DG, it appears to accelerate the reversion of LTP toward pre-potentiation levels. This may be a mechanism behind the impairment of memory consolidation by post-training 5-HT4 agonists. Thus, the timing of 5-HT4 of activation or inactivation is critical for determining whether there will be positive or negative effects on memory consolidation.

8.20 Although 5-HT4 agonism enhances cognitive processes, the loss of 5-HT4 in KO mice revealed no deficits in learning and memory. This is likely due to developmental adaptation and compensation of the ACh system to the absence of 5-HT4.

#### 9. [Serotonin4 (5-HT4) Receptor Agonists Are Putative Antidepressants with a Rapid Onset of Action: Neuron](https://www.cell.com/neuron/fulltext/S0896-6273\(07\)00618-6)

9.1 5-HT4 receptor agonists reduce immobility in the FST, showing antidepressant-like effects. 5-HT4 agonists modify rat brain parameters considered to be key markers of antidepressant action, and they do this with a 3d treatment. Classical antidepressants take 2-3w to show such signs. These include desensitization of 5-HT1A autoreceptors, increased tonus on hippocampal postsynaptic 5-HT1A receptors, and enhanced phosphorylation of CREB and neurogenesis in the hippocampus. In contrast, 3d treatment with Citalopram failed to show such an effect. 3d treatment with RS67333 was sufficient to reduce both the hyperlocomotion induced by olfactory bulbectomy (OBX) and the reduction of sucrose intake (a measure of anhedonia) induced by chronic stress. In the inhibition of stress-induced reduction of sucrose intake RS67333 had a much more rapid onset than Citalopram.

9.2 5-HT4 receptors play an excitatory role in the rat dorsal raphe nucleus (DRN), increasing neuronal firing activity. The DRN is virtually devoid of 5-HT4 mRNA and protein. The DRN-excitatory action is mediated by indirect control of the DRN by the mPFC. 5-HT4-mediated positive control is already at its maximum after 3d and maintains the same amplitude after 21d administration.

9.3 5-HT4 receptor agonists may act as rapid-acting antidepressants.

9.4 Stimulation of 5-HT4 receptors, after only 3d of treatment, is able to elicit the same functional, morphological, and molecular changes induced by classical antidepressants: desensitization of presynaptic 5-HT1A autoreceptors in the DRN, enhanced inhibitory tonus mediated by postsynaptic 5-HT1A in the hippocampus, increased phosphorylation of CREB in hippocampal tissue, and the induction of hippocampal neurogenesis.

9.5 Both Prucalopride and RS67333 strongly reduced time spent immobile in the FST to a similar extent (~50%): from 88.2s (control) to 43.4s and 42.7s, respectively. Both agents produced a stronger effect than Citalopram (62.6s). Concerning the influence on climbing, RS67333 was more potent than Prucalopride or Citalopram, as only its effect (+94%) reached statistical significance. RS67333 did not affect locomotor activity, but Prucalopride significantly increased locomotor activity by 78% in the OFT.

9.6 Acute IV administration of Citalopram inhibited the firing rate of 5-HTergic DRN neurons with an ED50 of 130ug/kg and an ED100 of 280ug/kg. 3d treatment with RS67333 1.5mg/kg shifted these dose-response curves to the right, reaching 226ug/kg and 430ug/kg, respectively. WAY-100635 (a 5-HT1A antagonist) had a dose-dependent excitatory effect in rats treated with either RS67333 1.5mg/kg/d or Prucalopride 2.5mg/kg/d. The effect was moderate in the Prucalopride group, reaching significance only after the highest cumulative IV dose of WAY100635 (500ug/kg), at 191% of baseline. In the RS67333 group, all 3 cumulative doses of 200, 300, and 500ug/kg strongly increased the firing activity of CA3 pyramidal neurons to 276%, 378%, and 445% of basal values, respectively. 3d treatment was sufficient to induce the apparition of a 5-HT1A-mediated tonic inhibitory effect on CA3 pyramidal neurons. 3d treatment with Citalopram did not affect these variables.

9.7 3d treatment with 5-HT4 agonists is sufficient to desensitize 5-HT1A autoreceptors in the DRN.

9.8 3d treatment with 5-HT4 agonists decreased basal activity of CA3 pyramidal neurons. 

9.9 Acute systemic injection of preferential 5-HT4 agonists increased hippocampal 5-HT efflux.

9.10 para-Chlorophenylalanine (pCPA), an irreversible 5-HT synthesis inhibitor, abolished the facilitatory action of WAY-100635 in rats treated with RS67333.

9.11 Acute Prucalopride at a dose of 1mg/kg is known to induce both a significant and selectively 5-HT4-dependent augmentation of DRN 5-HT neuron activity. Of the 38 pyramidal neurons recorded, 21 were unaffected by Prucalopride, whereas 17 (45% of them) were clearly inhibited, discharging at only 46% of predrug values. This inhibition was 5-HT1A-dependent. The involvement of 5-HT4 receptors in the action of Prucalopride was confirmed by the neuronal activity inhibition was abolished by a 5-HT4 antagonist.

9.12 Prucalopride and RS67333 increased p-CREB immunoreactivity and p-CREB/CREB ratio. The mean values of ratios for p-CREB/CREB were 0.18 in control, 0.3 for Prucalopride, and 0.37 for RS67333. RS67333 thus increased the ratio by 105%, whereas Prucalopride produced a trend-level 66% increase.

9.13 3d treatment with RS67333 significantly enhanced the number of BrdU+ cells in the subgranular zone (SGZ) of the DG. In the RS67333 group, the stereological count indicates that the volumetric density of BrdU+ cells increased to 128% of vehicle values (19,120 cells/mm3 vs 14,955 cells/mm3). Detailed morphological analysis of the histological sections revealed that these cells were grouped in clusters of 6-10 clones following RS67333, suggesting an acute induction of mitotic activity in these cells.

9.14 Acute RS67333 and acute Prucalopride reduced immobility time in the FST (–50%) to a greater extent than acute SSRIs (–25%). Prucalopride, however, also increased motor activity. It is possible this is mediated by the low-affinity interaction of Prucalopride with DA D4 receptors, as even a weak stimulation of D4 receptors has been reported to facilitate locomotion. Prucalopride 2.5mg/kg was predicted to have target engagement with D4 receptors, while having its maximal effect on 5-HT4.

9.15 Prucalopride can induce robust central effects at a dose as high as 5mg/kg IP, in a manner that still appears, on a behavioral level, to be selective for 5-HT4 (i.e., blocked by a 5-HT4 antagonist).

9.16 Electrophysiological, molecular, morphological, and behavioral changes previously specifically linked to long-term treatment with SSRIs are already present after only 3d when using 5-HT4 agonists.

9.17 The DRN-hippocampus 5-HT transmission was deeply modified by 5-HT4 agonism. The shift to the right in the ability of acutely-administered Citalopram to inhibit 5-HT neuron firing rate clearly suggests a decreased sensitivity of somatodendritic 5-HT1A autoreceptors. A subpopulation of ~50% of DRN 5-HT neurons, discharging at a low (<1Hz) basal frequency, is insensitive to 5-HT4 stimulation. The more rapid desensitization of 5-HT1A with 5-HT4 agonists vs SSRIs may be due to differing mechanistic characteristics: rather than a passive elevation of extracellular 5-HT levels due to reuptake inhibition and related to a slow diffusion within extracellular space, the activation of 5-HT neuronal firing rate elicited by 5-HT4 agonists should facilitate a true, active release of 5-HT within the DRN. After a 3d treatment with RS67333, 5-HT release was enhanced in DRN projection areas, more particularly the dorsal hippocampus. In the case of RS67333, the inhibitory tonus appeared to be stronger than 21d treatment with SSRIs, as a 5-HT1A antagonist was able to increase CA3 pyramidal neuron activity up to 440% of predrug values, whereas this disinhibition has been reported to reach a maximum of 200% after 3w of Paroxetine. The effect of Prucalopride was more modest, as a high cumulative dose of WAY100635 was needed to unveil the existence of the 5-HT1A-mediated tonus. The difference in amplitude between the effects of the two 5-HT4 agonists parallel their action on DRN neuron firing rates: Prucalopride enhanced firing by ~40-45%, whereas the effect of RS67333 reached 70%.

9.18 Both Prucalopride and RS67333 were able, after only 3d of chronic administration, to enhance the phosphorylation of CREB in hippocampal tissue. This effect had not previously been observed before 1.5-2w of administration. RS67333 produced a stronger effect than Prucalopride, paralleling electrophysiological results, but was also of higher amplitude (+105%) than the previously-reported increase induced by long-term SSRI treatment (+20-30%). A number of data suggest that 5-HT4 receptors located on pyramidal neurons may directly activate CREB. This could potentiate the indirect 5-HT1A agonist effect of 5-HT4 agonists. Even before this, it had been proposed that hippocampal 5-HT1A and 5-HT4 receptors exert a synergistic, facilitatory control on CREB activation.

9.19 3d treatment with RS67333 was able to facilitate hippocampal neurogenesis, as well as enhance cell division and proliferation in the SGZ.

#### 11. [Enhancement of place and object recognition memory in young adult and old rats by RS 67333, a partial agonist of 5-HT4 receptors](https://sci-hub.st/https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S002839080100123X)

11.1 Activation of 5-HT4 receptors enhances cognitive processes including learning and memory. 

11.2 When injected before the acquisition phase, RS67333 1mg/kg enhanced place and object recognition memory in young adult rats. In old rats, RS67333 10mg/kg improved place recognition when injected before the acquisition phase, and object recognition when injected before the acquisition or in the consolidation phase of information processing. These effects were abolished by the 5-HT4 antagonist GR12587 10mg/kg IP. 

11.3 5-HT4 receptors are highly expressed in the limbic system, mainly in the hippocampus, frontal cortex, and amygdala, areas that contain a high density of cholinergic neurons or terminals. ICV injection of 5-HT4 agonists enhances the release of ACh in the rat frontal cortex. 5-HT4 receptors are markedly reduced in the AD brain.

# A Comprehensive Subjective Profile of Usmarapride (SUVN-D4010): Efficacy, Tolerability, and User-Reported Dynamics

## Introduction and Contextual Baseline

The landscape of cognitive enhancement, neurorehabilitation, and neuropsychiatric symptom management is increasingly characterized by the exploration of highly selective, receptor-specific ligands. Among the most compelling novel agents currently transitioning from controlled clinical environments into the sphere of decentralized, advanced self-experimentation is Usmarapride, widely known in the developmental literature by its investigational code SUVN-D4010. Originally synthesized and advanced through the clinical pipeline by Suven Life Sciences, Usmarapride is classified as a potent, highly selective, orally active, and brain-penetrant partial agonist of the serotonin 5-HT4 receptor. While its primary clinical trajectory has focused rigidly on ameliorating the profound cognitive deficits associated with Alzheimer’s disease and, secondarily, the cognitive symptoms of schizophrenia, a robust, highly detailed secondary literature of subjective, anecdotal reports has emerged from the cognitive enhancement community.

This comprehensive report synthesizes an exhaustive phenomenological and pharmacological profile of Usmarapride, focusing strictly on real-world subjective effects, psychological benefits, physiological side effects, and tolerability dynamics. By filtering out promotional narratives, vendor-driven marketing, and purely theoretical speculation, this analysis isolates authentic user experiences to construct a grounded, empirical understanding of the compound's behavior in human subjects. The ensuing analysis serves to bridge the substantial gap between established preclinical data—such as Usmarapride’s documented ability to elevate cortical acetylcholine and shift amyloid precursor protein (APP) processing toward neuroprotective pathways —and the lived, daily experiences of individuals utilizing the compound for cognitive optimization, emotional modulation, and critical recovery from psychostimulant-induced neuroadaptation.

Through an exhaustive examination of user-reported dosages, onset timelines, sustainability metrics, and multi-compound synergies, this report maps the intricate ways in which targeted 5-HT4 receptor modulation translates into conscious human experience.

## Pharmacological Underpinnings of the Subjective Experience

To accurately interpret and contextualize the subjective reports surrounding Usmarapride, it is an absolute necessity to first understand how its highly specific mechanism of action translates from a molecular event into a conscious, phenomenological experience. The 5-HT4 receptor is an excitatory G-protein-coupled receptor (GPCR) that is positively coupled to the Gs protein. Upon activation, it stimulates the enzyme adenylyl cyclase, leading to a rapid and sustained increase in intracellular cyclic adenosine monophosphate (cAMP). This elevation in cAMP subsequently activates protein kinase A (PKA), which cascades into the phosphorylation of the cAMP response element-binding protein (CREB), a critical transcription factor fundamentally involved in neuroplasticity, memory consolidation, and the long-term structural health of neurons.

In the central nervous system, 5-HT4 receptors are not distributed uniformly; rather, they are densely and specifically localized in the hippocampus, the prefrontal cortex, and the amygdala. These are the precise anatomical regions intrinsically linked to episodic memory formation, executive function, decision-making, and emotional regulation. The specific localization of these receptors dictates the nature of the drug's effects. The subjective experiences consistently reported by independent researchers and users are downstream manifestations of several primary pharmacological events triggered by Usmarapride's interaction with these brain regions.

The first major event is direct cholinergic modulation. Usmarapride acts as a powerful heteroreceptor modulator, significantly enhancing the evoked release of acetylcholine in the cortex and the hippocampus. Subjectively, this acute cholinergic surge translates into reports of significantly enhanced working memory, heightened perceptual sharpness, an increased capacity to hold complex concepts in mind simultaneously, and an overall sense of cognitive "freshness". Unlike classical stimulants that force the release of dopamine and norepinephrine to create an artificial sense of urgency, the cholinergic enhancement provided by Usmarapride is frequently described as a smooth, frictionless access to existing cognitive reserves.

The second major pharmacological event is the profound modulation of the Default Mode Network (DMN). Strong empirical evidence from functional magnetic resonance imaging (fMRI) studies involving other 5-HT4 agonists, such as prucalopride, demonstrates a distinct and measurable reduction in neural activation within the network of regions corresponding to the DMN. The DMN is typically highly active during periods of mind-wandering, self-referential thought, and, pathologically, during the obsessive, depressive rumination that characterizes many mood disorders. Its acute suppression by 5-HT4 agonism aligns perfectly with ubiquitous user reports of experiencing a "lightness of being," an unburdened mental state, and a distinct, noticeable reduction in anxious, repetitive thought loops.

The third critical event relates to neurotrophic and neuroplastic enhancement. Preclinical evaluations demonstrate that Usmarapride significantly increases the levels of neuroprotective soluble amyloid precursor protein alpha (sAPPα), steering the brain's metabolism away from the toxic amyloid-beta (Aβ) peptides that form plaques in Alzheimer's disease. Furthermore, sustained 5-HT4 activation is deeply implicated in the long-term modulation and expression of Brain-Derived Neurotrophic Factor (BDNF). Subjectively, this neurotrophic cascade does not yield an immediate, acute psychoactive effect. Rather, it manifests as a progressive, structural stabilization of mood and cognitive baseline over weeks of continuous use. This structural healing is particularly noted and heavily relied upon by users attempting to recover from neurotoxic insults, such as long-term amphetamine abuse.

It is of paramount importance to recognize that Usmarapride is a partial agonist (with an EC50 reported variously around 44 nM or 27.5 nM) rather than a full agonist at the 5-HT4 receptor. This partial agonism is highly relevant to the subjective profile; it intrinsically possesses a pharmacological ceiling effect. This characteristic prevents the catastrophic overstimulation of the receptor, thereby mitigating the rapid receptor internalization (downregulation) and the severe, cascading side effects that are typically associated with full serotonergic agonists. This allows the compound to be used sustainably without the rapid onset of absolute tolerance.

## The Phenomenology of Default Mode Network Suppression

One of the most profound and consistently reported subjective effects of Usmarapride revolves around its ability to alter the user's fundamental perception of self and environment, an effect closely tied to the aforementioned suppression of the Default Mode Network. To truly understand the user experience, one must deeply analyze the specific vocabulary used in anecdotal reports.

Users frequently describe the acute effects of Usmarapride as inducing a profound "lightness of being," an experiential state that encompasses both the mind and the physical body. This is not described as the euphoric, manic high associated with dopaminergic compounds, but rather as an absence of psychological gravity. This lightness is frequently accompanied by reports of sudden, striking visual acuity. In a highly illustrative and pharmacologically significant anecdotal report, one user taking a standard dose of 15 mg of Usmarapride (in conjunction with an experimental TrkB modulator) explicitly compared the acute sensory and cognitive effects to "a small dose of ketamine without the anaesthetic effects". This specific comparison is highly revealing. Ketamine, a well-known NMDA receptor antagonist, is famous in both psychiatric literature and recreational communities for its rapid and aggressive suppression of the Default Mode Network, which leads to its rapid-acting antidepressant effects and a sense of detached, objective clarity. The fact that a targeted 5-HT4 partial agonist elicits a subjectively identical sense of mental "lightness," sensory vividness, and emotional detachment from rumination strongly supports the hypothesis that Usmarapride is acting profoundly to downregulate DMN hyperactivity. When the DMN is suppressed, the brain is less bound by rigid predictions and repetitive anxieties, allowing for the flexible, associative thinking that characterizes childhood cognition and deep creative states.

A significant point of divergence within the user base surrounds the optimal frequency of administration. ## Application in Psychostimulant Withdrawal and Neurorecovery

## Timeline of Efficacy: Onset, Sustainability, and Tolerance Dynamics

A critical consideration for the evaluation of any psychoactive compound is the trajectory of its efficacy over time, the rate at which the central nervous system adapts to its presence, and the physiological consequences of its sudden removal. In this regard, the data surrounding Usmarapride reveals highly favorable dynamics.

### The Dynamics of Tolerance

The human serotonergic system is highly dynamic, and its receptors are notoriously prone to rapid downregulation and desensitization in the continuous presence of exogenous full agonists. This phenomenon is exceptionally well documented in the enteric nervous system, where the continuous administration of older 5-HT4 full agonists often leads to a rapid, complete loss of prokinetic efficacy.

However, the dynamics of tolerance to Usmarapride in the central nervous system appear to be fundamentally and distinctly different, a fact largely attributed to its precise molecular design as a partial agonist. Partial agonists, by definition, possess a lower intrinsic efficacy at the receptor site than the endogenous ligand (in this case, native serotonin). Consequently, they do not trigger the same aggressive, overwhelming intracellular feedback loops that lead to the rapid phosphorylation, internalization, and ultimate degradation of the receptor complex.

Subjective user reports perfectly mirror this advanced pharmacological theory. Users engaging in the chronic/cumulative protocol (administering a daily dose of 5-10 mg) do not report a rapid, devastating loss of efficacy, nor do they report the dangerous need to continuously escalate the dosage to achieve the same baseline effects. The acute, hyper-vivid sensory effects (the initial "lightness" and intense visual acuity) may attenuate slightly over the first several continuous days of use as the brain adjusts to the new, elevated cholinergic baseline, but the core therapeutic benefits—enhanced working memory, profound mood stability, and DMN suppression—appear highly sustainable over long durations. For those specifically wishing to maintain the acute, ketamine-like sensory and emotional effects indefinitely, strict adherence to a twice-weekly dosing schedule completely prevents any subjective tolerance buildup whatsoever.

When taken as an oral liquid volumetric solution, which bypasses some of the delays of solid pill digestion , users consistently report the onset of acute subjective changes—such as visual acuity and cognitive lightness—within a rapid window of 30 to 60 minutes. One of the most profound and unique advantages of Usmarapride, as derived from the exhaustive review of the subjective literature, is the apparent, total absence of a distinct withdrawal syndrome upon cessation of the compound.

Unlike classical central nervous system stimulants—which predictably result in a devastating crash characterized by absolute dopamine depletion, severe lethargy, and suicidal anhedonia —or traditional SSRIs—which can result in a debilitating discontinuation syndrome featuring "brain zaps," severe vertigo, and violent emotional crashing due to sudden serotonin depletion —ceasing the administration of Usmarapride does not trigger a physiological or psychological crisis. ## Adverse Reactions and Tolerability Constraints

While the formal clinical data suggests a highly favorable safety and tolerability profile for Usmarapride , real-world supratherapeutic dosing and individual biological variability have elucidated several distinct side effects and tolerability constraints that must be navigated.

### Dose-Dependent Cephalalgia (Headaches)

The most consistently reported and limiting adverse effect of Usmarapride is the manifestation of acute headaches. Crucially, aggregated user data indicates with high confidence that this side effect is strictly dose-dependent and typically features a hard, predictable threshold. Multiple independent reports confirm that doses up to 10 mg or 15 mg are generally extremely well-tolerated, but escalating the dose beyond the 15 mg threshold frequently results in acute, sometimes severe, tension headaches.

The exact physiological etiology of these headaches is not explicitly defined in the anecdotal literature, but pharmacological deduction suggests two primary culprits. First, excessive, sudden surges in cortical acetylcholine can induce immense physical tension and rapid vascular changes in the brain, a phenomenon commonly and reliably observed with high doses of acetylcholinesterase inhibitors used in Alzheimer's therapy. Second, systemic 5-HT receptor activation is known to profoundly influence cerebral vasodilation and vasoconstriction. Users generally manage this side effect successfully by strictly and rigidly adhering to the 10-15 mg ceiling dose, never exceeding it.

### Gastrointestinal Tolerability: The Prucalopride Comparison

To fully and accurately understand the side effect profile of Usmarapride, it must be directly contrasted with older, first-generation 5-HT4 agonists, most notably prucalopride. The 5-HT4 receptor was initially discovered and researched for its role in the enteric nervous system, specifically its ability to vastly increase gut contractility, peristalsis, and motility. Approximately 95% of the human body's total serotonin is produced and utilized in the periphery, largely within the gut. Accordingly, 5-HT4 agonists like prucalopride are primarily prescribed by gastroenterologists for severe chronic constipation. Their use off-label by researchers for cognitive enhancement is severely, almost entirely limited by their primary and aggressive side effect: intense, unyielding diarrhea and profound gastrointestinal distress.

Usmarapride was specifically and intentionally engineered by medicinal chemists to circumvent this exact limitation. By possessing a high degree of blood-brain barrier penetrance and acting strictly as a partial rather than a full agonist, it achieves remarkable central nervous system selectivity while sparing the gut. Subjective reports strongly, unanimously confirm this intended pharmacological design. Users explicitly and frequently note the total absence of nausea, diarrhea, or peripheral gastrointestinal distress when using Usmarapride, setting it apart as a vastly superior, highly tolerable compound for long-term cognitive and psychiatric use compared to its predecessors.

### The Risk of Emotional Overwhelm

As previously noted in the benefits section, the rapid "emotional fluctuation" and unblunting induced by the drug can be a double-edged sword. For a user seeking simple, robotic, stimulant-like focus to complete mundane tasks (such as data entry), the sudden, unexpected resurfacing of deeply buried grief, loneliness, or intense joy can be highly disruptive to workflow and subjectively classified by the user as a severe adverse effect.
