Hi, my name is Ewan. I'm a senior Computer Science undergrad at the [[University Of Vermont]], working toward a PhD in [[Computational Neuroscience|computational neuroscience]]. I'm interested in how [[In Silico|in silico]] models can make the brain [[Mechanistic Interpretability|interpretable]], and how much we can learn from sparse, everyday signals of cognition.

Modern deep networks are exceptionally good at predicting brain responses to stimuli, partly thanks to advances in [[Representation]]s of text, images, and video. I'm curious about what these models have learned, and what probing them might teach us about the brain. Even models trained to predict classical single-neuron recordings [@ladLettingNeuralCode2026] offer a new medium for [[Mechanistic Interpretability|interpreting]] circuits en masse. Highly recommend that paper.

These models can also be used to design exploratory, almost *adversarial* stimuli, using neuroimaging feedback to guide new experiments. Well-funded academic research [@hendersonDiffusionbasedStimulusOptimization2026] has piqued my interest in pushing this further, guiding deep learning research toward real neural representations. The same approach can tell us about the content [[Representation]] models themselves [@princeParametricNeuralControl2026]: if two models predict responses to natural stimuli equally well, what new stimuli would distinguish them? On a much smaller scale, I've been doing experiments with more open tools like [[TRIBE v2]], and impractical ones like [[Maximizing Activation through Differentiable Content Consumption]].

As an undergrad with limited funding (my entire disposable income), I've taken these questions to sparse, everyday signals. It's fun to think about how much of our internal brain state is already [[Partially Observable Markov Decision Process|partially observable]]: what you say out loud or what video you're watching might offer clues about what you're thinking [@wuInversePOMDPInferring]. As fun as it is to apply evolutionary algorithms to [[TRIBE v2]] inputs, it is a population-level model, and an fMRI setup is one of the few things my lab of a bedroom doesn't have. Instead I start with two devices: a mediocre 4-channel [[BrainAccess HALO]] (after trying to [[3d Printing|3d print]] my own) headset, and a [[Tobii Eye Tracker 5|Tobii ET 5]], a gaming peripheral which will fight you on using it as a research [[Using an Eye Tracker for more computer data|eye tracker]].

There's also what we already have. With the amount of [[Consuming and Choosing Content Effectively|content we consume]], I find interest in obsessively [[On Capturing Personal Data|recording personal data]] as behavioral sources: [[Tracking EVERYTHING I do on my computer|computer activity]] ([[ActivityWatch]]), [[On Transcribing Audio Recordings|transcription/diarization of speech]], and the writing timesink that is this [[Digital Garden]] vault (+git timeseries data). The same goes for [[Health]] and [[Longevity]] data; a large part of my work relies on wearable data for tracking physiology and the effects of interventions. Each stream is an incomplete view, but they are views of the same underlying state, aligned by timestamp. I want to know how much of a [[Mapping The Mind|shared representation can be learned from several poor sensors together]], and what each one adds.

Further out, I'm interested in using this model to test causal interventions against benchmarks ([[Anki Telemetry for Self Configuration Efficacy|Anki]], [[Self Configuration For Chess|Chess]]): shaping content diet, and even extensions into other methods of [[Self Configuration]] (for me that is [[Nootropic Compounds.base|neuropharma]], for most just stick to what you already consume).

At UVM, I work with speech data and transcription/diarization, post-training speaker representation models to better capture acoustic features that predict cognitive impairment. My room is always a lab and every kind of lab: [[Saturn V Server Rack|homelab]] as the compute for [[On Capturing Personal Data|Quantified Self]] data work and collection en masse, [[3d Printing]] and ESP32s for everything, and a mini pharma shelf for pursuing the ever diminishing returns of health optimization.
